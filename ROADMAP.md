# UI/UX Track Roadmap

> One roadmap, two levels. **Beginners** and **Intermediates** follow the same sessions, answer the same task questions, and build toward the same final project. What changes is the depth: Beginners get guided tasks, templates, and more checkpoints. Intermediates answer the same questions but must justify their decisions with evidence and alternatives. By the end of the season, everyone reaches a similar level.

---

## How this roadmap works

- **One session per week**, 15 sessions plus a 5-week final project (roughly 4 to 5 months).
- **One running brief** (Weeks 2 to 15): design a website for a student tech community. Every task is one piece of that brief.
- **Screen critique (15 minutes)** in every session: one member's screen is reviewed by the group using the topic of the week.
- **Materials:** each session has Core resources (everyone) and Deep Dive resources (Intermediates, optional for Beginners). Items marked (Arabic) are in Arabic.
- **Tasks:** one shared task per week, with a Beginner version and an Intermediate version.
- **Final Project:** a team project that follows the full design process, from problem research to a case study.
- All materials are free. Last link check: October 2026.

## Levels

| | Beginner | Intermediate |
|---|---|---|
| **Overall** | Basic or developing understanding of UI/UX. May be completely new. | Has hands-on UI/UX practice and understands the core concepts. |
| **Experience** | None or limited. No projects or case studies required. | Has worked on projects or case studies and can explain their role. |
| **Thinking** | Can think about users and problems with some guidance. | Can explain and justify design decisions. |
| **Figma** | Never used it, or knows the basics. | Comfortable with Figma, including Auto Layout, Components, Variables, or Design Systems. |
| **In this roadmap** | Guided tasks, templates, and more checkpoints. | Same questions, deeper justification, higher standards, more independence. |

## Week 1

**Session:** Intro, Design Thinking & Figma Basics
**What we cover:** What UI and UX are, how the design process works (Design Thinking), and getting comfortable in Figma.

**Materials**
- **Core:** [Learn Design by Figma](https://www.figma.com/resources/learn-design/getting-started/) (free, self-paced)
- **Core:** [Figma for beginners, Part 1: Explore ideas](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/sections/23691657321239-Courses) (official course)
- **Core:** [Design Thinking (Arabic)](https://ehabfayez.com/learn/design-thinking)
- **Deep Dive:** [The 5 Stages of the Design Thinking Process (IxDF)](https://ixdf.org/literature/article/5-stages-in-the-design-thinking-process%e2%80%8b)
- **Deep Dive:** [User-Centered Design (Arabic)](https://ehabfayez.com/learn/user-centered-design)

**Task (both levels):** Pick an app you use daily and find 3 UX problems in it. Set up your season Figma file (pages, naming, structure).

- **Beginner:** Follow the course to build a Profile Card. Use the provided template for the 3 problems (what, who it affects, why).
- **Intermediate:** Rank the 3 problems by severity and justify the ranking. Name which Design Thinking stage each problem belongs to.

**Screen critique (15 min):** review one screen using this week's topic.

## Week 2

**Session:** Product Thinking
**What we cover:** User goals vs business goals, writing a problem statement, and How Might We questions.

**Materials**
- **Core:** [UX problem-solving frameworks: Problem Statements, HMW, 5 Whys (Arabic)](https://ehabfayez.com/learn/problem-solving-ux)
- **Core:** [Using How Might We Questions (NN/g)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-might-we-questions/)
- **Deep Dive:** [User-Centered Design (Arabic)](https://ehabfayez.com/learn/user-centered-design)

**Task (both levels):** For the running brief (a website for a student tech community), write: user goals, community (business) goals, one problem statement, and 3 HMW questions.

- **Beginner:** Use the provided problem statement template and examples. Mentor checkpoint before moving on.
- **Intermediate:** Show where user goals and community goals conflict, and justify which one wins. Explain why your HMW questions are neither too broad nor too narrow.

**Screen critique (15 min):** review one screen using this week's topic.

---

## Week 3

**Session:** Research Methods
**What we cover:** Choosing the right method and when to use it: interviews, surveys, observation.

**Materials**
- **Core:** [User Interviews: How, When, and Why (NN/g)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/user-interviews/)
- **Core:** [UX Research guide (Maze)](https://maze.co/guides/ux-research/)
- **Core:** [UX Research (Arabic)](https://ehabfayez.com/learn/ux-research)
- **Deep Dive:** [Research Methods (NN/g topic page)](https://www.nngroup.com/topic/research-methods/)
- **Deep Dive:** [Tips for New UX Researchers: Remote Interviews (NN/g video)](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/tips-new-ux-researchers-remote-interviews/)

**Task (both levels):** Plan and run research for the brief: write the research questions, choose a method, and interview students about how they find and join tech communities.

- **Beginner:** Interview 2 students using the provided script. Write down what you learned in your own words.
- **Intermediate:** Run 4 to 5 interviews and a short survey. Justify why you chose these methods over the alternatives, and state what each method cannot tell you.

**Screen critique (15 min):** review one screen using this week's topic.

## Week 4

**Session:** Synthesis: Insights, Personas & Journey Map
**What we cover:** Turning raw research into insights, personas, and a journey map.

**Materials**
- **Core:** [User Personas (Arabic)](https://ehabfayez.com/learn/user-persona)
- **Core:** [Journey Mapping 101 (NN/g video)](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/journey-mapping-101/)
- **Deep Dive:** [Design Thinking: Define stage (Arabic)](https://ehabfayez.com/learn/design-thinking)

**Task (both levels):** From your research, build an affinity map, one persona, and one journey map for joining the community.

- **Beginner:** Use the provided persona and journey map templates. Highlight 3 pain points.
- **Intermediate:** Back every persona trait and pain point with a quote or data point from your research. Mark the biggest opportunity and explain why.

**Screen critique (15 min):** review one screen using this week's topic.

---

## Week 5

**Session:** User Flow, IA & Feature Prioritization
**What we cover:** Organizing content, mapping how users move, and deciding what to build first.

**Materials**
- **Core:** [Information Architecture (Arabic)](https://ehabfayez.com/learn/information-architecture)
- **Core:** [Information Architecture: Study Guide (NN/g)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ia-study-guide/)
- **Core:** [5 Prioritization Methods in UX Roadmapping (NN/g)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/prioritization-methods/)
- **Deep Dive:** The card sorting and tree testing articles inside the IA Study Guide

**Task (both levels):** Create a sitemap, one user flow for joining the community, and a prioritized feature list.

- **Beginner:** Draw one flow and prioritize features with an impact-effort matrix.
- **Intermediate:** Add flows for visitor, applicant, and member. Run a card sorting with 3 people. Prioritize with a second method (for example RICE or MoSCoW) and explain any difference between the two results.

**Screen critique (15 min):** review one screen using this week's topic.

---

## Week 6

**Session:** Wireframing, UX States & UX Writing
**What we cover:** Turning flows into structure, designing every state of a screen (empty, loading, error, success), and writing clear interface text.

**Materials**
- **Core:** [Wireframing (Arabic)](https://ehabfayez.com/learn/wireframing)
- **Core:** [UX Writing: Study Guide (NN/g)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-writing-study-guide/)
- **Core:** [Learn Design by Figma](https://www.figma.com/resources/learn-design/getting-started/) (wireframing lessons)
- **Deep Dive:** Free wireframe kits on [Figma Community](https://www.figma.com/community)

**Task (both levels):** Wireframe the Home, Tracks, and Join screens. Include the empty, error, and success states of the Join form, with real interface text instead of lorem ipsum.

- **Beginner:** Low-fidelity wireframes for the 3 screens, one state per screen, using the provided checklist.
- **Intermediate:** Wireframe the full sitemap for mobile and desktop, with all states. Add 2 alternative layouts for Home and a note explaining your choice. Justify your wording choices.

**Screen critique (15 min):** review one screen using this week's topic.

## Week 7

**Session:** Typography & Color
**What we cover:** Making visual choices that are consistent, readable, and accessible, including Arabic type.

**Materials**
- **Core:** [Typography (Arabic)](https://ehabfayez.com/learn/typography)
- **Core:** [Color Theory (Arabic)](https://ehabfayez.com/learn/color-theory)
- **Core:** [WebAIM Contrast Checker](https://webaim.org/resources/contrastchecker/)
- **Deep Dive:** [Material Design 3: Styles](https://m3.material.io/styles)
- **Deep Dive:** [Apple Human Interface Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/) (Color and Typography)

**Task (both levels):** Choose fonts and a color palette for the community website and apply them to your Home wireframe. Check contrast.

- **Beginner:** Choose 2 fonts and one palette. Check text contrast with the checker.
- **Intermediate:** Define a type scale and color roles (primary, secondary, semantic) with a dark mode palette. Justify each choice by the brand and users, and show contrast results.

**Screen critique (15 min):** review one screen using this week's topic.

## Week 8

**Session:** Spacing, Grids & Visual Hierarchy
**What we cover:** Guiding the eye with spacing, alignment, and hierarchy.

**Materials**
- **Core:** [Layout and Grid Systems (Arabic)](https://ehabfayez.com/learn/layout-grid-systems)
- **Core:** [Learn Design by Figma](https://www.figma.com/resources/learn-design/getting-started/) (layout and composition)
- **Deep Dive:** [Material Design 3: Foundations](https://m3.material.io/foundations)
- **Deep Dive:** [Apple Human Interface Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/) (Layout)

**Task (both levels):** Build a high-fidelity Home page using an 8pt spacing system and a column grid.

- **Beginner:** Apply the 8pt system and a 12-column grid with the provided guide.
- **Intermediate:** Redesign a page with weak hierarchy and explain 3 changes with before and after, tied to a specific principle.

**Screen critique (15 min):** review one screen using this week's topic.

## Week 9

**Session:** Auto Layout
**What we cover:** Building layouts that resize and adapt on their own.

**Materials**
- **Core:** [Guide to auto layout (Figma Learn)](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040451373-Guide-to-auto-layout)
- **Core:** [Figma Guide (Arabic)](https://ehabfayez.com/learn/figma-guide)
- **Deep Dive:** The nested auto layout section of the guide above

**Task (both levels):** Rebuild your Home page navigation and card list with Auto Layout so they grow and shrink with content.

- **Beginner:** Build a navigation bar and a card list with Auto Layout.
- **Intermediate:** Use nested Auto Layouts (Hug, Fill, min/max) for a full page that needs no manual fixes. Explain why you chose each resizing setting.

**Screen critique (15 min):** review one screen using this week's topic.

## Week 10

**Session:** Components & Variants
**What we cover:** Building reusable pieces with states.

**Materials**
- **Core:** [Build design systems (Figma Learn)](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/sections/23536356509975-Build-design-systems)
- **Core:** [Figma for beginners, Part 2: Create designs](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/sections/23691657321239-Courses)
- **Deep Dive:** [Figma Guide: Components (Arabic)](https://ehabfayez.com/learn/figma-guide)

**Task (both levels):** Create the components the website needs (Button, Input, Card, Navigation) with variants for their states.

- **Beginner:** Create a Button with 3 states (Default, Hover, Disabled) and a Card.
- **Intermediate:** Build the full component set with variants and component properties. Justify your naming and how you decided what becomes a component.

**Screen critique (15 min):** review one screen using this week's topic.

## Week 11

**Session:** Variables & Design Systems
**What we cover:** Managing values in one place and combining styles, components, and rules into a system.

**Materials**
- **Core:** [Design Systems (Arabic)](https://ehabfayez.com/learn/design-systems)
- **Core:** [Use variables in prototypes (Figma Learn)](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/14506587589399)
- **Core:** [Introduction to design systems (Figma course)](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/sections/23691657321239-Courses)
- **Deep Dive:** [Material Design 3](https://m3.material.io/get-started) as a real-world design system

**Task (both levels):** Turn your colors, type, and spacing into Variables and publish a mini design system for the website.

- **Beginner:** One-page style guide plus color variables.
- **Intermediate:** Mini design system as a Figma library with light and dark modes, documentation, and a clear token naming scheme that you can defend.

**Screen critique (15 min):** review one screen using this week's topic.

## Week 12

**Session:** Responsive Design
**What we cover:** Making one design work across screen sizes.

**Materials**
- **Core:** [Responsive Design (Arabic)](https://ehabfayez.com/learn/responsive-design)
- **Core:** [Guide to auto layout (Figma Learn)](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040451373-Guide-to-auto-layout) (resizing behavior)
- **Deep Dive:** [Material Design 3: Foundations](https://m3.material.io/foundations)

**Task (both levels):** Adapt the Home and Join pages to mobile and desktop.

- **Beginner:** Mobile and desktop versions of the Home page.
- **Intermediate:** Mobile, tablet, and desktop with breakpoints. Explain how the navigation and content priority change at each one, and why.

**Screen critique (15 min):** review one screen using this week's topic.

## Week 13

**Session:** Prototyping
**What we cover:** Turning static screens into an interactive experience.

**Materials**
- **Core:** [Prototyping and Interaction (Arabic)](https://ehabfayez.com/learn/prototyping-interaction-design)
- **Core:** [Figma for beginners, Part 3: Build prototypes](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/sections/23691657321239-Courses)
- **Deep Dive:** [Use variables in prototypes (Figma Learn)](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/14506587589399)

**Task (both levels):** Build a clickable prototype of the Join flow, including the error and success states.

- **Beginner:** A clickable prototype of 5 to 6 screens.
- **Intermediate:** Add Smart Animate and Variables (for example form states). Justify where animation helps users and where it does not.

**Screen critique (15 min):** review one screen using this week's topic.

## Week 14

**Session:** Usability Testing & Accessibility
**What we cover:** Checking whether the design works for real people, and for all people.

**Materials**
- **Core:** [Usability (User) Testing 101 (NN/g)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-testing-101/)
- **Core:** [10 Usability Heuristics (NN/g)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/)
- **Core:** [Usability Testing (Arabic)](https://ehabfayez.com/learn/usability-testing)
- **Core:** [Accessibility and Inclusivity (Arabic)](https://ehabfayez.com/learn/accessibility-inclusivity)
- **Deep Dive:** [Why You Only Need to Test with 5 Users (NN/g)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/why-you-only-need-to-test-with-5-users/)
- **Deep Dive:** [User Testing: Why & How (NN/g video)](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/user-testing-jakob-nielsen/)

**Task (both levels):** Write a test script with tasks, test your prototype with users, and list the problems you found.

- **Beginner:** Test with 3 people on 3 tasks using the provided script. List the top 3 problems.
- **Intermediate:** Write a test plan and test with 5 users. Measure success rate and time, rate problems by severity using the heuristics, check accessibility, and iterate with before and after.

**Screen critique (15 min):** review one screen using this week's topic.

## Week 15

**Session:** AI Tools in UI/UX
**What we cover:** Using AI to speed up work while you stay in charge of the decisions. What each tool is for, and how to write a good prompt.

**Materials**
- **Core:** [AI Tools for Designers (Arabic)](https://ehabfayez.com/learn/ai-tools-designers)
- **Core:** [Prompting for Designers (Arabic)](https://ehabfayez.com/learn/prompting-for-designers)
- **Core:** [Generating and Critiquing UI with AI (Arabic)](https://ehabfayez.com/learn/ai-ui-generation)
- **Deep Dive:** [AI in UX Research (Arabic)](https://ehabfayez.com/learn/ai-in-ux-research)

**Task (both levels):** Use at least two tools from the table below on one screen or flow from your project. Write the prompt you used, what you changed, and what AI got wrong.

- **Beginner:** Generate one screen variation with one tool and write what worked and what did not.
- **Intermediate:** Compare two tools on the same prompt. Document which decisions were yours, which AI mistakes you fixed, and when you would not use AI.

**Screen critique (15 min):** review one screen using this week's topic.

**AI tools: what each is needed for**

| Tool | Needed for | Free plan (checked October 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| [Figma Make](https://www.figma.com/solutions/ai-design-generator/) | Turns a prompt or a design into an interactive prototype | Check current limits; they change by plan |
| [Google Stitch](https://stitch.withgoogle.com) | Fast UI ideas for mobile and web (beta) | Limits not stated on the page; check before relying on it |

| [Base44](https://base44.com) | Complete apps with a built-in database | Free plan: 25 message credits per month, up to 5 apps |

Free limits change often. Check the tool page before the session.

## Final Project (Weeks 16 to 20)

A **team project** for each group, with the same brief and the same steps for both levels. Beginner teams get templates and more mentor checkpoints. Intermediate teams are expected to go deeper in research, testing, and the design system.

| Step | What you do | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Problem Research | Understand users and define the problem | Research summary and problem statement |
| 2. Wireframes & Design Process | Explore solutions and structure them | Wireframes and design decisions |
| 3. Final UI | Build the polished interface | Final UI and mini design system |
| 4. Prototyping | Make it interactive | Clickable prototype |
| 5. Case Study | Tell the story of the project | Case study |

**The Case Study covers:** UX Process, Design Decisions, and Results & Learning.

**Helpful resource:** [Figma for beginners, Part 4: Prepare for handoff](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/sections/23691657321239-Courses)
